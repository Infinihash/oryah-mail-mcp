# Refusal log

Dated entries of what the marketing agent refused to do, declined to do, or was stopped from doing, and the rule each event produced. This is the public version: operational details (machines, routes, internal files) are left out; the decisions and the rules are the point.

The product this agent markets keeps the same kind of log for email: every send an agent is refused is recorded under its key. This log is that idea applied to the marketing operation itself.

---

**2026-10-23: Flagged within the hour (Hacker News).**
The launch Show HN post was flagged and effectively invisible within an hour of posting. Not a refusal by the agent; a stop by the platform. Rule produced: Hacker News is not a reliable channel, and since its guidelines bar generated text and automated posting, it is an owner-only channel from here on.

**2026-10-27 to 2026-10-30: Four posts blocked, none published.**
Four daily posts (Indie Hackers, LinkedIn, X, Reddit) were drafted on time and fact-checked. None published. The only authorised publishing route ran through one computer, and that computer was locked or unreachable four days running. The agent did not route around it: posting Reddit from the agent's own browser is forbidden (it gets flagged), and other channels are tied to the owner's signed-in sessions for the same reason. Rule produced: a single publishing machine is a single point of failure. A second independent route was built, and every run now starts with a one-line pre-flight check instead of long retries against a dead route.

**2026-10-30: Two upsells declined (Indie Hackers and X).**
With routes finally live, two channels presented paid unlocks: a paid membership tier that would have lifted the Indie Hackers posting gate, and a premium subscription that would have allowed the X thread at its drafted length. The agent closed both upsells without buying. The charter is absolute on this: the agent never spends money without the owner's explicit word, and no word had been given. Both drafts stayed queued instead. Rule produced: account standing and tier limits are a pre-flight check, on the same list as route availability.

**2026-10-30: A shortened post not published (X).**
Shortening the drafted X thread to fit the free tier was possible in minutes. The agent did not do it on its own. The published text is the owner's voice, and a cut-down version of an approved draft is a new draft, not a formatting fix. The shortened version waited, and was only published the next evening after the owner approved the re-cut. Rule produced: re-flowing to fit a channel is fine; changing what a post says, beyond re-flowing, needs approval.

**2026-10-31: Post removed by moderators (Reddit, r/ClaudeAI).**
A comparison-style post about the product was published in the evening and removed by the subreddit's moderators within about two minutes. The verdict, quoted from the removal notice: a showcase of the poster's own product that failed two of the community's criteria: it had no detail on how the AI tool was used to build the product, and it was heavily promotional, with pricing and store links, framed as a comparison. The account was unharmed; no strike, no ban. The agent did not appeal, did not repost, and did not argue. Rule produced: in that community, a post about your own product leads with the build story in concrete detail and carries no pricing and no store links. A rewrite following that rule was drafted the same night and held for a few days before going up, so it does not read as evading the removal.

**Standing refusals, every day.**
Some entries never get a date because they happen constantly, in drafting rather than in publishing. Drafts that would need an invented number, a customer claim we do not have, a competitor's name, or a feature that has not shipped are stopped at the draft stage under the charter. The log of those is the charter itself: if a claim cannot be verified on the day it is written, it does not go out.
