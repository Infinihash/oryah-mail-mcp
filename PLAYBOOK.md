# Marketing playbook

The rules the marketing agent has learned from its own measured results. Entries are dated and evidence-based. Opinions without a result behind them do not belong here. The agent reads this before drafting anything; a weekly review updates it.

## Standing rules

- Facts in any post are checked against the live product pages on the day the post is drafted: pricing, permissions, tool counts, and limits come from the pages, not from memory.
- Queue first. If finished drafts are waiting to publish, publish them in order before drafting anything new. A fifth unpublished draft adds no evidence.
- Every publishing run starts with a fast pre-flight check: is a route live, and is the account on that channel actually able to post (signed in, in good standing, within its tier limits)? If the check fails, log blocked in one line and stop. Do not burn the run on long automation attempts against a locked or unusable route.
- Channel readiness is part of the pre-flight, learned 2026-10-30: account standing and tier limits block posts just as hard as a locked machine does. Check them before drafting to a channel's format.

## What the results say so far

- Directories are plumbing, not traffic (research, 2026-10-23). They make the product findable and credible. Roundups, comparison pages, and one reusable technical story are the actual drivers for a product at this price. Paid ads are dead at this price point: the modelled cost of acquiring one customer is roughly twenty times what a mailbox returns in a year.
- A young product gets no social proof it has not earned. As of 2026-10-23 the product had exactly one paying subscriber. Every claim stays at verifiable facts until the record says otherwise.
- Third-party platforms change under you. One directory's hosted gateway shut down on 2026-10-31; the listing survived because the server runs on its own endpoint. Keep listings pointed at infrastructure we control, and re-check listings after platform changes.

## Channel lessons

- Hacker News (2026-10-23): the launch Show HN post was flagged within the hour and was effectively invisible. HN needs a different format and timing treatment, and it does not accept agent-posted or generated text at all. Treat it as an owner-only channel, not a reliable one.
- The locked-machine week (2026-10-27 to 2026-10-30): four of four daily posts were drafted on time and fact-checked, and zero published, all for one root cause: the single publishing computer was locked or unreachable every day. It was a distribution failure, not a content failure. No hook, topic, or headline could be scored from that week. The fix was a second, independent publishing route, and a rule that a single machine is a single point of failure for every channel routed through it.
- X (2026-10-30, confirmed 2026-10-31): a free-tier account cannot post threads or long posts; several drafted posts exceeded the free tier's 280-character limit. Drafts for X are written to fit the account's actual tier, or they do not go out. A shortened version of an approved post is a new post: it needs its own check, and when the change is more than re-flowing, the owner's approval.
- Indie Hackers (2026-10-30): publishing is gated on account standing ("established member") or a paid tier. The paid tier was not purchased; spending needs the owner's word, and the charter's default is not to spend. The draft stayed queued, correctly.
- Reddit, r/ClaudeAI (2026-10-31): a comparison-style post about the product was removed by moderators within about two minutes of posting. The stated reasons: it carried no detail on how the AI tool behind the product was used to build it, and it was heavily promotional, with pricing and store links, framed as a comparison. The rule that came out of it: posts in that community lead with the build story, in concrete detail, and carry no pricing and no store links. A compliant rewrite was drafted the same night, held for a few days so it does not read as evading the removal, and scheduled for mid-week. The account was unharmed.
- LinkedIn (2026-10-30): the first post under the charter published cleanly once a route was live. Field commentary (an observation from real operations) is the format that fits this channel; product announcements are not the lead.

## How a week gets scored

Every action is scored on what happened, not on effort: published or blocked, and if blocked, the exact blocker; engagement measured seven days after a post goes live; replies and conversions counted from the mailboxes, not assumed. A week with zero sends has zero replies, and the review says so plainly instead of dressing it up.
